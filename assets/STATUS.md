## Custom rig pipeline (Nib, Hod, Loom)

- `tools/blender/rig.py` builds a skeleton from a JSON spec, weights the mesh, adds keyed clips and writes a GLB. Runs headless in Blender 5.2.
- Tested end to end on Flint's unrigged mesh with a made-up 11-bone skeleton (`tools/blender/test-spec.json`): result loads in the viewer with textures and a working clip.
- Finding: Blender's automatic weighting fails directly on Meshy meshes. The script falls back to weighting a voxel copy and transferring the weights, which left no unweighted vertices.
- Nib is rigged with this script from `assets/nib/work/rig.json`: joints measured from the mesh, leg reach by IK, clips keyed by hand.
- Nib's clips are a first pass: one clip each for bite, upward strike, tail sweep and pounce is shared across his moves; tails and ears have no secondary motion yet.
- Hod and Loom are rigged the same way; their specs are generated by `tools/blender/companions.js` because their two sides are mirrored.
- Loom shows its flat face to the camera. Its clips treat +X as forward and the game mirrors the model when it turns round (`faceCamera` in the manifest).
- Each companion now has nine to eleven attack clips, so ground attacks, smashes, aerials and specials mostly look different. A few moves still share a clip (see `assets/manifest.json`).
- Follow-through: tails, ears and ribbons are simulated in `render3d.js` (`springs` in the manifest). They trail when the fighter moves and settle when it stops. It is stepped by the game clock, so freezing the game freezes it.
- Still stand-ins for the companions: roll, dodge, air dodge, grab hold, being held and the ledge hang reuse other clips.
- Not checked: every state of every companion in the game. Idle and one attack each were looked at; the rest use the same mechanism.
